# Commercial use

The software in this repository is licensed under
[PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0](LICENSE), and the model weights under the
[VNLM Community License 1.0](MODEL_LICENSE.md). Both allow noncommercial use only.
**To use either one commercially, you need a written license from dotori.ai.**

## Free, no approval needed

- Personal use: study, hobby projects, private entertainment.
- Research and experiments, including at a company, as long as nothing is offered for
  commercial gain and there is no product or internal business use.
- Teaching, and any use by a school, university, public research institute, charity,
  public-health or public-safety organization, or government body.
- Publishing papers, benchmarks and noncommercial demos built on the project.
- Writing in Extended Korean, implementing the EK encoding, and using the EK charts,
  language packs and fonts. These are open; see [LICENSES.md](LICENSES.md).

## Needs a commercial license

- Shipping the software, the models, or anything derived from them in a product or paid
  service, including SaaS, APIs, apps, devices and on-premise deployments.
- Using them inside a for-profit organization's operations (customer support,
  transcription, translation, data processing), even if nothing is sold.
- Training, fine-tuning or distilling a model for commercial use from the VNLM weights,
  or from the outputs of the VNLM weights.
- Offering hosted access to the models, paid or free, as part of a commercial service.
- Bundling the software or the models with a commercial product.

If you are not sure whether your use is commercial, ask. A short email costs nothing,
and the answer is often "go ahead".

## How to request a license

Write to **licensing@dotori.ai** with:

1. Your organization and a contact person.
2. What you want to build, and which parts you want to use (code, models, packs).
3. The languages and markets involved.
4. Rough scale: users, requests, or devices.

Licenses are offered on reasonable terms. Free or discounted terms are available for
startups below a revenue threshold, for nonprofits, and for language-preservation work.
Some of the income pays the linguists who build the language packs.
